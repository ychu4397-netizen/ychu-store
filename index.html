Week 2: Analysing YouTube's User Experience

One website I use regularly is YouTube. In my experience, one of its main strengths is its powerful search functionality. The keyword suggestions make it easier to find relevant videos, even when I do not know their exact titles. The website also presents video lists and comments in a relatively clear way, helping users explore content and interact with others.

However, I sometimes find YouTube's interface visually overwhelming. As a video-sharing platform, it presents a large amount of content through thumbnails, titles and other visual elements. Many thumbnails use striking images and colours to attract attention. Although this can make videos more noticeable, it can also make it difficult for users to concentrate on their original goals. For example, someone who visits YouTube to find a particular educational video may become distracted by other content.

This observation made me think about the relationship between visual design and attention. A website can provide a great deal of information, but more content does not necessarily create a better user experience. In my view, effective design should establish a clear visual hierarchy so that users can identify the most important information without feeling overwhelmed.

This also connects to Gillespie's (2013) discussion of how algorithms determine the relevance of information. Search suggestions and content recommendations can influence how users discover and engage with content. Although these features can make a platform more convenient, it is also worth considering how the organisation and presentation of information shape users' experiences.

Reflecting on my own portfolio website, I realised that I face a similar design challenge. I want to present a variety of information and eventually showcase my work, but including too much content without clear priorities could make the website difficult to navigate. Since I do not yet have many projects to display, I would like to focus on communicating my personal style and interests first, before gradually developing the portfolio section.

At the same time, I recognise that YouTube and a personal portfolio website serve different purposes. YouTube needs to help users discover and watch a large amount of video content, whereas a portfolio website should guide visitors towards the information and work that best represent its creator. Therefore, good design does not simply mean making a website more visually attractive or adding more content. It means organising information in a way that supports the website's purpose and helps users focus on what matters to them.

Reading: Gillespie, T. (2013). “The Relevance of Algorithms.” In T. Gillespie, P. J. Boczkowski, and K. A. Foot (eds.), Media Technologies: Essays on Communication, Materiality, and Society, pp. 167–193. MIT Press.
