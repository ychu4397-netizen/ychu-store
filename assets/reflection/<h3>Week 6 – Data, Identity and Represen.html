Week 6 – Data, Identity and Representation

The Week 5 workshop helped me realise that data does not speak for itself. Decisions about what to collect, how to classify it and what to leave out all shape the patterns we see. The purpose of collecting data also affects how we interpret it: the same data may be useful for one question but incomplete for another.

Sumpter’s analysis of his Facebook friends showed me how data can reveal patterns, while also simplifying people into categories. This connects with Cheney-Lippold’s idea of algorithmic identity: platforms can use fragments of our data to assign identities that may not match how we understand ourselves.

I also thought about the ethics of collecting and presenting data, especially consent, privacy and the risk of identifying people. Creating a visualisation was challenging because I wanted it to be clear without making the data seem more complete or certain than it was. I learned that categories and visual choices shape the story a dataset tells.

Readings: Cheney-Lippold, J. (2017). “Introduction.” In We Are Data: Algorithms and the Making of Our Digital Selves, pp. 1–32. New York: NYU Press.

Sumpter, D. (2018). “The Principal Components of Friendship.” In Outnumbered: From Facebook and Google to Fake News and Filter-bubbles: The Algorithms That Control Our Lives, pp. 25–34. London: Bloomsbury Sigma.
